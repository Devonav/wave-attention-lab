Hardware measurements for this repository. Raw data is
[`rx9070xt-fp32.csv`](rx9070xt-fp32.csv); kernel traces are in
[`profiles/`](profiles).

Junction temperature peaked at 72 C. GPU busy was 99-100% for all variants.

## Method

`B=1`, `H=8`, FP32, non-causal, deterministic inputs. Three complete trials of
the standard matrix, 10 warmup and 100 timed iterations each, timed with HIP
events around the kernel launches only. Host/device copies and allocation are
outside the timed region. Each figure below is the median across the three
trials of that trial's own median. Correctness was checked against the CPU
oracle for every algorithm at both causal settings; the tiled path was
additionally checked at non-power-of-two sequence lengths (129, 511) and at head
dimensions that are not multiples of the wavefront (32, 96). Maximum absolute
error was at most `9.69e-08`.

## What the measurements show

**The fused path is 1.95x to 7.08x slower than the materialized baseline at every
shape.** This contradicts the framing the repository carried before there was
hardware data, where the fused online-softmax kernel was presented as the
optimization and the materialized path as the thing to beat. Caching Q in LDS is
close to a no-op on top of that.

**The tiled path is 2.63x to 7.25x faster than fused-cached** and is the fastest
custom kernel at seven of eight shapes. It keeps the fused path's `O(1)` score
workspace, so it beats the materialized baseline on memory as well as on time.

**Tiled loses to materialized at the smallest shape**, `N=128 D=64`, at 0.86x.
There is not enough work there to cover the tiling overhead: at `N=128` with a
32-key tile each block runs only four tile iterations, and the three simple
kernels of the materialized path saturate the card just as well. For `N >= 256`
tiled leads by 1.12x to 1.91x.

**The gap to PyTorch SDPA narrowed from 7.9-24.4x to 2.38-4.65x.** SDPA is still
ahead everywhere, which is expected against a hand-written FP32 kernel that makes
no use of the matrix cores.

## Profiler evidence

`rocprofv3 --kernel-trace`, `N=1024`, `D=64`, 2 warmup and 10 timed iterations,
all four variants from the same build.

| Variant | Kernel | Dispatches | Mean | Share |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| materialized | `score_kernel` | 12 | 5.1497 ms | 77.1% |
| materialized | `apply_value_kernel` | 12 | 1.4110 ms | 21.1% |
| materialized | `softmax_kernel` | 12 | 0.1190 ms | 1.8% |
| fused | `fused_attention_kernel` | 12 | 28.0336 ms | 100.0% |
| fused-cached | `fused_cached_attention_kernel` | 12 | 27.4642 ms | 100.0% |
| tiled | `tiled_attention_kernel` | 12 | 3.5133 ms | 100.0% |

Absolute times under the profiler include the warmup dispatches and profiling
serializes dispatches, so they differ from the benchmark medians and vary
with how warm the card is. The shares and the ranking are the usable signal.

- **Materialized** is dominated by `score_kernel` at 77.1%. It assigns one thread
  per `(query, key)` pair and walks the head dimension serially, so each thread
  re-reads a full Q row from global memory and neighbouring threads stride
  through K by `dimension` floats. Neither Q nor K is reused through LDS.
- **Fused** spends everything in one kernel whose structure is the problem. It
  launches one 256-thread block per query row and loops over keys *serially*,
  performing a full 256-thread tree reduction with `__syncthreads()` at each of
  the `log2(256) = 8` steps for every key. At `N=1024` that is 8192 barriers per
  query row. Caching Q removes a global load that was never the constraint.
- **Tiled** replaces both. One wavefront serves one query row, so the score
  reduction is a butterfly `__shfl_xor` inside the wave and needs no barrier at
  all. K and V are staged in LDS a 32-key tile at a time and reused by all eight
  waves in the block, leaving two barriers per tile: 64 per block at `N=1024`.

The power and clock traces corroborate the diagnosis. `fused-cached` runs about
four times longer than `materialized` while drawing a flat ~200 W against
materialized's spiky 83-338 W, which is what waves parked on barriers rather than
issuing work look like. The tiled kernel draws the same ~200 W but finishes far
sooner, and it is the only variant that pulls the memory clock up, to 1124 MHz
against 772-875 MHz for the others. It is the one moving data rather than waiting.

## What this implies for the rest of Milestone 2

Three of the Milestone 2 items are now done and measured: query tiling, LDS
tiling of K/V, and wave-level reductions. What the current numbers suggest next:

- **`D=128` gains much less than `D=64`**, 2.63-3.38x over fused-cached against
  5.91-7.25x. The LDS tile is statically sized for the maximum supported head
  dimension, so a `D=64` run reserves the same 32 KiB as a `D=128` run and gives
  up occupancy for nothing. Sizing the tile to the actual head dimension is the
  next experiment.
- **Tiled p95 sits well above its median**, 5.97 ms against 4.02 ms at
  `N=1024 D=64`, a wider spread than the other variants show. Worth understanding
  before tuning further.
- Aligned vector loads and FP16/BF16 inputs remain untested. Neither is likely to
  be the next large win on its own; the matrix cores are where the remaining
  distance to SDPA lives.

## Revision note

An earlier version of this report was measured on kernel `7.1.9-arch1-2` and
published a single trial per cell while claiming three, because `summarize.py`
overwrote each trial with the next instead of aggregating. Both are fixed: the
