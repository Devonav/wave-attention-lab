# RX 9070 XT FP32 Baseline

First hardware measurements for this repository. Raw data is
[`rx9070xt-fp32.csv`](rx9070xt-fp32.csv); kernel traces are in
[`profiles/`](profiles).

Clock, power, and thermal disclosure: `Performance Level: auto`, stock settings,

| Variant | `sclk` | `mclk` | Package power | GPU busy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | ---: |

Junction temperature peaked at 72 C. The memory clock held a low DPM state in
both variants for the whole sampling window, so neither path is pushing memory
bandwidth hard enough to make the controller ramp.

## Method

`B=1`, `H=8`, FP32, non-causal, deterministic inputs. Three complete trials of
the standard matrix, 10 warmup and 100 timed iterations each, timed with HIP
events around the kernel launches only. Host/device copies and allocation are
outside the timed region. Each figure below is the median across the three
trials of that trial's own median; per-trial p95 is in the CSV. Correctness was
checked against the CPU oracle for every algorithm at both causal settings;
maximum absolute error was at most `9.69e-08`.

## What the measurements show

**The fused path is slower than the materialized baseline at every shape**, by
1.84x to 6.37x. This contradicts the framing the repository carried before there
was hardware data, where the fused online-softmax kernel was presented as the
optimization and the materialized path as the thing to beat. On this card, for
these shapes, the opposite holds. The fused path's `O(1)` score workspace is real
and worth up to 32 MiB at `N=1024`, but it is currently paid for with latency
rather than earning it.

**Caching Q in LDS is close to a no-op.** `fused-cached` beats `fused` by 0.9% to
8.2%, with a median of 2.1%. The hypothesis that repeated Q loads were a
meaningful cost is not supported.

**The custom kernels are 7.9x to 24.4x slower than PyTorch SDPA.** The gap is
widest at `D=64`, where the fused kernels leave three quarters of each block idle.

## Profiler evidence

`rocprofv3 --kernel-trace`, `N=1024`, `D=64`, 2 warmup and 10 timed iterations.
Shares are of total kernel time within each variant.

| Variant | Kernel | Dispatches | Mean | Share |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| materialized | `score_kernel` | 12 | 10.1482 ms | 81.0% |
| materialized | `apply_value_kernel` | 12 | 2.1594 ms | 17.2% |
| materialized | `softmax_kernel` | 12 | 0.2205 ms | 1.8% |
| fused | `fused_attention_kernel` | 12 | 39.7815 ms | 100.0% |
| fused-cached | `fused_cached_attention_kernel` | 12 | 39.1208 ms | 100.0% |

Absolute times under the profiler are higher than the benchmark medians because
these means include the warmup dispatches and profiling serializes dispatches;
the shares and the ranking are the usable signal.

The bottleneck differs by variant:

- **Materialized** is dominated by `score_kernel` at 81.0%. It assigns one thread
  per `(query, key)` pair and walks the head dimension serially, so each thread
  re-reads a full Q row from global memory and neighbouring threads stride
  through K by `dimension` floats. Neither Q nor K is reused through LDS.
- **Fused** spends everything in one kernel whose structure is the problem. It
  launches one 256-thread block per query row and loops over keys *serially*,
  performing a full 256-thread tree reduction with `__syncthreads()` at each of
  the `log2(256) = 8` steps for every key. At `N=1024` that is 1024 sequential
  block-wide reductions per query row. The `d`-dimension parallelism is also
  wasted: at `D=64` only 64 of 256 threads do useful work in the accumulate step.

The power traces corroborate this. `fused-cached` runs about four times longer
than `materialized` while drawing a flat ~200 W against materialized's spiky
83-338 W. Taking four times the time at roughly two thirds the power is the
signature of a kernel whose waves are parked on barriers rather than issuing
work, which is what the serialized per-key reduction predicts.

This also explains why the Q-caching experiment did not pay off. It removes a
global load that was never the constraint; the barrier chain is.

## What this implies for Milestone 2

The two items already at the top of the Milestone 2 list, LDS tiling of K/V and
wave-level reductions, are the ones the profile points at. Since the card is
wavefront 32, a reduction that stays inside one wave removes the `__syncthreads()`
chain entirely.

The list was missing the larger structural fix, now added ahead of them: stop
assigning one block per query row, and tile queries so each block amortizes its
K/V loads across several queries. That is what closes the gap to SDPA; the other
two are refinements on top of it.

## Revision note

An earlier version of this report was measured on kernel `7.1.9-arch1-2` at
commit `5e1be9b` and has been superseded. Two things changed:

The measurements were re-taken on `7.2.3-arch1-3` after a reboot, so the running
and installed kernels now agree. Kernel time moved by under 5% at every shape
with `N >= 256`; the two smallest-shape cells moved more, but those are sub-0.16
ms measurements where run-to-run noise dominates. No conclusion changed.

More significantly, `summarize.py` was reporting only the last trial of each
cell rather than aggregating across them, so the previous report published a
single trial while claiming three. For one cell the three trials were 0.162,
0.136, and 0.120 ms and the report showed 0.120 ms, the fastest of the three,
which is exactly what the gate in [README.md](README.md) forbids. The numbers
here are medians across trials. This shifted several figures and widened the
headline fused-versus-materialized range from 1.9-4.0x to 1.84-6.37x.
